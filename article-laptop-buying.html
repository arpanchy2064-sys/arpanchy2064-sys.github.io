LAPTOPS • BUYING GUIDE

How to choose a laptop for coding and everyday use

Updated September 2026 • 7 min read

Buying a laptop can be confusing because manufacturers advertise many specifications at once. The easiest approach is to identify your workload first and then compare the components that affect it.

1. Start with the processor

The CPU handles many general computing tasks. For coding, web browsing, office work and study, a modern mid-range processor is usually a sensible starting point. Look beyond the processor name and check its generation, core configuration and power class.

2. RAM matters for multitasking

RAM affects how many applications and browser tabs you can keep active comfortably. For a modern Windows laptop, 16 GB is a useful target for coding and general multitasking. If you buy less, check whether the memory can be upgraded.

3. Choose an SSD

An SSD makes starting Windows, opening applications and accessing files much faster than a traditional hard drive. 512 GB is a practical capacity for many users, although large games and video projects can require more.

4. Think about the display

Resolution, brightness, refresh rate and panel quality all affect the experience. For study and coding, a comfortable, sufficiently bright display is often more important than an extremely high refresh rate.

5. Check ports and connectivity

Before buying, check for USB-A, USB-C, HDMI, an audio jack and the wireless standards you need. USB-C capabilities vary, so check the manufacturer's specifications if charging or display output through USB-C matters to you.

6. Don't ignore the battery and weight

A powerful laptop can be useful but may be heavier and have shorter battery life. Choose based on whether you mostly use it at a desk or carry it to school, college or work.

Final checklist

  • Modern CPU appropriate for your workload
  • 16 GB RAM if your budget allows
  • 512 GB or larger SSD
  • A comfortable display
  • Ports you actually need
  • A battery and weight suitable for your routine

Tip: Compare the exact model number, not just the laptop series name. Two laptops with the same series name can have very different CPUs, screens, RAM and storage.
